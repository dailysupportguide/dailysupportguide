# Botanical Label Plant Part and Form: What to Copy Before Comparing

Botanical labels can include several details in a small space: a name, plant part, form, amount, and serving size. If those details are mixed together too quickly, the comparison gets blurry.

This article explains what to copy before comparing botanical labels. It does not rank products, recommend brands, interpret medical use, or replace guidance from a licensed professional.

## Start with the botanical name

FDA consumer information explains that dietary ingredients include herbs and other botanicals. FDA supplement labeling guidance says Supplement Facts must list the names and quantities of dietary ingredients present.

Start by copying the botanical name exactly as shown. If the label includes a common name and another name, keep both in the note instead of choosing one.

## Copy the plant part

FDA dietary supplement labeling guidance says the part of the plant from which a dietary ingredient is derived must be included in the Supplement Facts panel for dietary supplements.

Plant part should be its own field. Leaf, root, flower, seed, fruit, bark, and other plant-part words should not be blended into a general ingredient note.

## Keep form separate from plant part

A label may also include form wording, such as powder, extract, oil, or another descriptor. Form is not the same as plant part.

For cleaner notes, use separate fields: botanical name, plant part, form, amount, unit, serving size, and panel location. That keeps the comparison from leaning on one word too heavily.

## Check the panel and serving size

FDA supplement labeling guidance says the nutrition label for a dietary supplement is called a Supplement Facts panel. The panel must list serving size and, when required, servings per container.

Before comparing two botanical lines, copy the serving size. A botanical amount tied to one capsule is not the same serving basis as an amount tied to two capsules or one scoop.

## Do not fill in missing botanical details

If the label does not show a detail you expected, write "not shown" or "unclear" instead of filling it in from memory. A missing detail is a comparison note, not proof of quality or fit.

If a personal question depends on the botanical, plant part, amount, medication use, pregnancy, breastfeeding, allergy, surgery, or medical condition, follow licensed professional guidance.

## Use the same fields for each label

A simple botanical note can use the same fields every time: botanical name, plant part, form, amount with unit, serving size, and panel location. If a label includes a blend, keep the blend total separate from individual ingredient details.

The point is not to make the article decide which product is better. The point is to keep label facts organized enough to compare.

## A practical takeaway

Botanical labels are easier to read when botanical name, plant part, form, amount, and serving size stay in separate fields. This article is general education only and is not medical advice.
