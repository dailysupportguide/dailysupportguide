# Everyday Food Rhythm Production Plan

## Purpose

This plan defines the third 50-article content line after the label-reading, nutrient-note, and body-signal work.

Working title: Everyday Food Rhythm

Editorial promise: help readers build ordinary meal, grocery, snack, and kitchen rhythms without diet rules, brand examples, product recommendations, medical advice, or moralizing language.

## Scope

- 50 English articles.
- Five existing site authors, 10 articles each.
- Draft in 5-article batches.
- Each batch must pass internal polish before external review.
- External review quorum remains ChatGPT + Gemini + Claude.
- Grok is not part of the required gate.
- Do not approve, schedule, publish, commit, or push directly from drafting unless the user explicitly authorizes that step.

- Theo Grant: comparison notes editor; practical, source-aware, note-sheet driven.

## Series Safety Rules

This series should stay firmly in everyday planning and observation.

- Do not create diet plans, weight-loss plans, medical nutrition therapy, or personalized food rules.
- Do not moralize foods as clean, toxic, good, bad, forbidden, or guilt-free.
- Do not recommend products, stores, apps, brands, supplements, tests, doses, or tracking devices.
- Do not make diagnosis, treatment, cure, prevention, blood-sugar, hormone, detox, metabolism, or performance claims.
- Use food rhythm, grocery rhythm, pantry notes, meal timing, meal structure, and weekly reflection framing.
- Keep personal health factors separate: pregnancy, breastfeeding, medication use, allergies, eating disorder history, chronic conditions, surgery, and individualized nutrition plans.
- End each article with the required wording: "This article is general education only and is not medical advice."

## Anti-AI Voice Rules

- Avoid template openings across articles.
- Avoid generic wellness language such as "optimize", "unlock", "journey", "empower", "holistic", and "transform".
- Avoid identical section rhythm across a batch.
- Avoid over-polished reassurance.
- Prefer ordinary scenes: late breakfast, half-used groceries, desk snacks, shared dinners, travel weeks.
- Keep author voice visible without becoming gimmicky.
