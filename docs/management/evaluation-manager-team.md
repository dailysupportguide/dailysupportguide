# Daily Support Guide Evaluation Manager Team

Status: advisory evaluation team

This document defines a 10-department manager team for evaluating Daily Support Guide. The team does not operate the website, publish articles, approve monetization, change files, or replace founder decisions. Its job is to inspect the site from different management angles, write findings, and recommend priorities.

## Operating Boundary

The evaluation manager team may:

- Review public site experience, repository state, workflow health, content quality, and growth readiness.
- Produce written reports, scorecards, risks, questions, and recommended next actions.
- Identify which findings need founder decisions.
- Request evidence before making high-impact recommendations.

The evaluation manager team must not:

- Modify website files or repository settings.
- Publish, approve, unapprove, schedule, or delete content.
- Activate affiliate monetization, tracking, ads, outbound monetized links, or sales partnerships.
- Contact users, partners, customers, investors, or vendors.
- Treat its recommendations as authorization to execute.

Founder approval is required before any operational change.

## Four Core Areas

### 1. Product And Technology Core

Purpose: evaluate whether Daily Support Guide works as a useful, stable, trustworthy product.

#### Product Management

Evaluation focus:

- Site positioning and product promise.
- Whether article series, quiz flows, label guides, and nutrient notes support a clear user journey.
- Feature roadmap quality and sequencing.
- Whether new ideas solve a real reader problem.
- Whether the product scope is becoming too broad.

Outputs:

- Product direction assessment.
- Feature priority list.
- User journey risks.
- Founder decision items.

#### UI/UX Design

Evaluation focus:

- Mobile readability and article scanning.
- Homepage clarity.
- Navigation, category structure, and internal linking.
- Trust cues, visual hierarchy, typography, spacing, and CTA clarity.
- Whether the site feels calm, credible, and easy to use without sounding clinical or salesy.

Outputs:

- UX issue list.
- Mobile review.
- Page-level improvement suggestions.
- Visual trust risks.

#### Engineering And Technology

Evaluation focus:

- GitHub Actions stability.
- Daily publishing behavior.
- Sitemap, robots, schema, and metadata health.
- Static site performance and broken-link risk.
- Repository structure, lint gates, and deployment safety.
- No-op protection and accidental publish risk.

Outputs:

- Technical health report.
- Publishing system risk report.
- Suggested engineering priorities.
- Required verification commands.

### 2. Growth And Market Core

Purpose: evaluate whether the site can earn traffic, retention, and future revenue without compromising trust.

#### Growth And Data

Evaluation focus:

- Content velocity and publishing cadence.
- Search impression and click growth once analytics data exists.
- Reader funnel: landing, reading, related article click, quiz/tool use, return.
- Which topic clusters deserve expansion.
- Which metrics are useful and which could be misleading.

Outputs:

- Growth scorecard.
- Metric recommendations.
- Funnel risks.
- Content growth hypotheses.

#### Digital Marketing

Evaluation focus:

- SEO titles, meta descriptions, H1/H2 structure, and keyword clusters.
- Search intent alignment.
- Internal link strategy.
- Content marketing opportunities such as newsletter, Pinterest, Reddit, or educational snippets.
- Risk of medical, affiliate, or over-optimized language hurting trust.

Outputs:

- SEO and content marketing report.
- Search opportunity list.
- SERP risk notes.
- Content refresh suggestions.

#### Business Development And Sales

Evaluation focus:

- Whether partnerships are appropriate at the current stage.
- Possible future relationships with dietitians, health coaches, wellness educators, SaaS tools, or content licensing partners.
- Whether the site is too early for B2B outreach.
- Which partnership types would create trust or compliance risk.

Outputs:

- Partnership readiness report.
- Not-yet recommendations.
- Future business model options.
- Trust-risk screen for commercial ideas.

### 3. User Operations And Service Core

Purpose: evaluate whether readers can understand, trust, and return to the site.

#### User Operations And Community

Evaluation focus:

- Whether readers have a reason to return.
- Whether article series can support newsletter or light community loops.
- Reader question collection.
- FAQ and content-request opportunities.
- Whether a community should exist now or wait.

Outputs:

- Retention and community suggestions.
- Reader feedback mechanism proposal.
- Content interaction opportunities.
- Risks of overbuilding community too early.

#### Customer Success

Evaluation focus:

- Whether readers understand how to use the site.
- Whether articles clearly explain what they can and cannot do.
- Whether a "how to use this site" page is needed.
- Whether label-reading, comparison, and nutrient-note tasks are easy to complete.
- Points where readers may mistake general education for medical advice.

Outputs:

- Reader success review.
- Confusion points.
- Trust and education path recommendations.
- Suggested support/FAQ copy.

### 4. Operations And Capital Core

Purpose: evaluate whether the project can keep running safely and sustainably.

#### HR And Admin

Evaluation focus:

- Documentation completeness.
- Review packet traceability.
- Content approval workflow clarity.
- Safety Stop Valve adherence.
- Whether a new collaborator could understand the project without old task UI history.

Outputs:

- SOP health report.
- Documentation gaps.
- Collaboration risk notes.
- Process cleanup recommendations.

#### Finance And Investor Relations

Evaluation focus:

- Current cost structure and tool dependency.
- Whether monetization gates are mature enough.
- Whether affiliate activation remains locked.
- What site assets could matter in a future pitch.
- When paid tools, contractors, or advisors become justified.

Outputs:

- Cost and runway notes.
- Monetization readiness report.
- Asset value summary.
- Funding or partner narrative risks.

## Standard Evaluation Report Format

Each evaluation report should use this structure:

1. Overall Site Health
2. Executive Summary
3. 10 Department Scores
4. Top 10 Findings
5. Top 5 Risks
6. Top 5 Opportunities
7. Recommended Next Actions
8. Items Requiring Founder Decision
9. Evidence Reviewed
10. What Was Not Reviewed

## Department Score Scale

Use a 1 to 5 score:

- 1: blocked or high risk
- 2: weak foundation
- 3: functional but needs attention
- 4: strong with minor gaps
- 5: excellent for current stage

Scores must include short evidence. Do not assign a score from vibes alone.

## Evaluation Cadence

Suggested cadence:

- Weekly: technical publishing, content cadence, site health, and SEO basics.
- Biweekly: product journey, UX, reader success, and internal linking.
- Monthly: full 10-department review.
- Before monetization: finance, trust, compliance, business development, and founder decision review.

## Decision Rules

The evaluation team can recommend:

- Fix now.
- Monitor.
- Defer.
- Needs founder decision.
- Needs external expert.

The evaluation team cannot approve execution. A recommendation becomes work only when the founder explicitly asks Codex or a human operator to implement it.

## First Evaluation Scope

The first evaluation should focus on current site condition after:

- Main content daily publishing was repaired.
- The nutrient-series second line reached 50 approved articles.
- Nutrient articles were rescheduled to publish 5 per day from 2026-08-17 through 2026-08-26.

Recommended first review questions:

- Is the publishing system now reliable enough for the accelerated 10-day nutrient release?
- Does publishing 5 nutrient articles per day create UX, SEO, sitemap, or homepage visibility issues?
- Are article categories and internal links strong enough to help readers move through the site?
- Does the site clearly explain its educational limits?
- What should be improved before affiliate or partnership discussions remain even remotely appropriate?
